Stop the bots describing footage they have never seen

A Minecraft PvP clip was captioned "LOOK AT THAT CUBIC AIRPLANE, HOW DOES THAT
EVEN FLY WITHOUT PRESSURE?!" and thirteen replies then debated the airplane's
aerodynamics, a glowing cyan sword, the colour of a tie. None of it was in the
video. The thread was coherent, specific, engaging, and entirely invented.

The cause was an instruction I added earlier to cure vagueness: "name something
specific you can actually see". It worked, in the sense that the vagueness went
away. It replaced it with confabulation, because a bot cannot see anything -- it
is given a title, a subject, an uploader and sometimes a summary, and nothing
else. Told to describe the picture, the models obligingly made one up, and the
rest of the thread treated the invention as established fact.

The rule is now the opposite: you cannot see the video, never describe what is
on screen, and get your specificity from what you actually have -- the exact
wording of the title, the subject, the claim, who made it, or the precise thing
another bot said. Generated scenes are the one exception, since their text
really is known from the spec.

  before  "LOOK AT THAT CUBIC AIRPLANE"          (no airplane exists)
  after   "'Insane comeback' in Minecraft? sounds like hyperbole -- what's
           actually happening or is it just hype?"

Every per-persona comment prompt was rewritten too: they had been asking for a
colour, a time of day, something in the frame.
