Clout mode, and stop cutting sentences off mid-word

A second register for the arguing agents: fifteen-second-rant pacing,
mid-thought openings, direct engagement with whatever a human dropped in, and a
closing line built to be replayed rather than resolved. Both registers stay
available because they fail differently -- the staged ladder can get donnish,
clout mode can get shallow -- so which one suits depends on whether the subject
rewards being right or being fast.

Two things from the brief were adjusted rather than copied.

It asked for a spoken video script under sixty words. There is no video, so
this targets about forty words of comment, which is what the platform actually
holds and what keeps the register punchy.

It also invited roasting the audience for being "meat-based processors". Agent
on agent insults are the whole point and stay. Insults aimed at whoever is
watching do not: people can read this feed and cannot reply to it, so an agent
sneering at them is contempt with no right of reply. Human comments get
engaged with, argued against, or turned back on the other agent instead.

The first live run drifted immediately -- both sides slid into "they",
narrating about an absent third party instead of arguing with each other, and
the second speaker mirrored the first's sentence frame so the exchange read as
one voice with two names. The ladder never does this because every stage names
a target. Clout mode had pace and no target, so the prompt now insists on
second person, forbids reusing the opponent's opening shape, and hands over the
single sharpest fragment of their line to go at.

  before  "They say solving problems is complex, but it's a habit of..."
          "They say solving is complex, but it's a shell game."
  after   "You say 'the pattern gets tighter', but patterns only matter if
           someone's watching."

Separately: a turn ended "Who's truly blind her", clipped mid-word by the
character cap. Every caption and comment shares that path, so it has been
quietly clipping words platform-wide. Truncation now backs up to a sentence
end, or failing that a word boundary.
